# Brewery tap list software in 2026: the honest buyer's guide

You've got a chalkboard behind the bar, a rotating 12-tap system, and a customer base that asks "what's on?" 40 times a night. You're ready to go digital. Here's the real comparison of what's on the market right now — features, pricing, and the things the sales pages don't tell you.

We'll cover four options that brewery owners actually mention: **Untappd for Business**, **DigitalPour**, **Taplist.io**, and a few honorable mentions. No affiliate links. No kickbacks. Just what we've learned from talking to brewery owners.

## The four real options

**Untappd for Business** is the brand-recognition play. Most beer drinkers already use the Untappd consumer app, so putting your menu on Untappd feels like a free distribution channel. The tradeoff: **price.** Pricing isn't published, but brewery owners report plans starting around **$899–$1,199 annually** ([Taplist.io comparison page](https://taplist.io/compare/untappd-for-business-alternative)), and the product is now owned by Next Glass, a private-equity-backed holding company. Expect price hikes. Expect your menu to look like every other Untappd venue. That's the deal.

**DigitalPour** is the taproom-focused option. Strong in beverage-specific workflows — ABV, IBU, keg-level tracking, TV display mode. Pricing is more transparent: **$400–$700 per location per year** ([Taplist.io vs DigitalPour](https://homepage.taplist.io/digitalpour/)). Good fit if you're deeply draft-oriented and want something built specifically for beverage programs. The interface shows its age in places.

**Taplist.io** is the scrappy newcomer — founded 2019, clear pricing, **about half the cost of Untappd for Business**, month-to-month billing, and a hard focus on "your brand, not ours" ([Taplist.io](https://taplist.io)). The product has matured fast. If you care about your taproom's visual identity and don't want the Untappd-template look, this is the one people mention most.

**Honorable mentions:** BeerMenus (consumer-facing beer discovery, lighter on brewery features), Evergreen (bar-focused digital menus), and rolling-your-own with a Google Sheets + TV solution. [Evergreen has a solid alternatives roundup here](https://www.evergreenhq.com/blog/untappd-alternatives-best-digital-beer-menu-solutions/).

## What to actually compare

Pricing gets all the attention, but the things that matter 12 months in are usually not what gets you signed up. Here's the checklist:

**1. Does it run on a TV? How?**
Every vendor claims "TV display mode." The question is *how.* A browser-based display that auto-refreshes is ideal — just point a cheap Fire Stick at your URL and go. Proprietary apps that require dedicated hardware are a red flag. Ask specifically.

**2. Who owns the menu data?**
If you ever want to leave, can you export your beers? Your tap history? Your customer-facing menu URL? Some vendors make leaving expensive. Ask before you sign.

**3. Does it integrate with your POS?**
If your bartender has to update the menu in two places every time a keg kicks, you're doubling labor. The best platforms sync with Square, Toast, Arryved, or your existing POS. If yours doesn't, factor the manual-update time into your "true cost."

**4. How fast is "real-time"?**
"Live tap list" can mean anything from 5-second updates to "we push at 2am." If your menu rotates during service (it probably does), you want seconds, not hours.

**5. Embed-ability on your website.**
Your brewery website should have a live tap list on it. Not a link to a third-party page. Not a screenshot you update weekly. An **iframe or embed widget** that pulls the same data as the TV display, so you update once and it's everywhere. Ask for the embed.

**6. Multi-location support.**
Even if you have one taproom today, if you're planning a second, ask about **brand-level menus** that roll up across locations. This becomes non-negotiable the day you sign a second lease.

## The pricing you won't see on the website

Here's the dirty secret of the tap list software market: **list prices are meaningless.** Vendors routinely offer discounts for multi-year commitments, concessions during migration, and price-matching if you mention a competitor by name. Always ask for a discount. Always get the price in writing. Never auto-renew without re-negotiating.

## Our take

If you want the Untappd consumer-app audience piped into your taproom and price isn't a factor, **Untappd for Business** is the obvious pick. If you're a beverage-first taproom and want something deeply beer-focused, **DigitalPour** earns its fans. If you want the best feature-per-dollar in 2026 and care about your own brand showing up first, **Taplist.io** is the one we'd point a friend at today.

All three will beat a chalkboard.

## One more thing

Whichever you pick, **start with a 90-day review on your calendar.** Did the menu actually stay current? Did customers use it? Did your POS sync cleanly? Did you get a support response when something broke? Most brewery owners set it up, walk away, and never look again. Six months later they're paying for a product that isn't working as intended and nobody noticed. Put the review on the calendar now.
